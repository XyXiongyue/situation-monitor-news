Gold Rises as U.S.-Iran Fighting Pauses, Markets Await Federal Reserve Decision

Sun, 26 Jul 2026 17:02

Gold gained more than 1% as easing U.S.-Iran tensions pushed oil prices lower and weakened the dollar, while investors awaited the Federal Reserve's interest rate decision.

Gold prices rose sharply on Monday as easing tensions between the United States and Iran triggered a steep decline in crude oil prices, reducing inflation concerns and boosting demand for the precious metal ahead of the Federal Reserve's interest rate decision. Spot gold climbed 1.4% to $4,110.56 per ounce, while U.S. gold futures gained 1% to $4,112.10.

The rally followed Iran's pledge to suspend military operations as long as the United States maintained its pause in airstrikes. Brent crude and West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude both fell more than 5%, easing fears of prolonged disruptions around the Strait of Hormuz, through which the U.S. Energy Information Administration estimates roughly 20 million barrels of crude oil and petroleum products about 20% of global petroleum liquids consumption normally transit each day.

With lower oil prices reducing immediate inflation risks and the U.S. dollar weakening, investors turned to gold while awaiting signals from the Federal Reserve on the future path of interest rates.

Gold benefited from simultaneous declines in oil prices and the U.S. dollar, two factors that typically support bullion prices.

The U.S. Dollar Index fell 0.3%, making dollar-denominated gold less expensive for overseas buyers and improving international demand. At the same time, lower energy prices reduced inflation expectations, encouraging investors to scale back bets on aggressive monetary tightening.

Because gold does not generate interest income, expectations of lower interest rates or declining real yields generally improve its relative attractiveness compared with interest-bearing assets such as government bonds.

The sharp decline in crude prices followed Iran's announcement that it would halt attacks as long as the United States maintained its suspension of military operations, providing markets with the strongest sign yet of a possible de-escalation.

Although no formal ceasefire has been reached, the temporary pause reduced fears of supply disruptions in the Persian Gulf. According to the International Energy Agency, the Strait of Hormuz also carries around 20% of global liquefied natural gas trade, making stability in the region critical for global energy markets.

Federal Reserve Decision Becomes Key Market Driver

Investor attention is now centered on the Federal Reserve's policy meeting later this week.

Markets broadly expect the Federal Open Market Committee to leave interest rates unchanged, but futures markets continue to anticipate the possibility of another increase later this year. According to the CME FedWatch Tool, traders are pricing in roughly an 80% probability of a rate hike at the September meeting.

Any indication that policymakers intend to delay further tightening could weaken Treasury yields and the dollar, creating a more favorable environment for gold. Conversely, a more hawkish outlook could strengthen the dollar and limit further gains in bullion.

Gold Faces Technical Resistance Amid Policy Uncertainty

Despite Monday's rally, traders continue monitoring important technical resistance levels near $4,200 an ounce.

Gold has remained supported by strong central bank purchases and geopolitical uncertainty throughout 2026. According to the World Gold Council, global central banks have continued to diversify reserve holdings by increasing gold purchases, providing long-term support for prices even as monetary policy expectations fluctuate.

Silver and Other Precious Metals Extend Gains

The broader precious metals complex also advanced alongside gold.

Silver extended gains above $55 per ounce, while platinum and palladium also traded higher as declining Treasury yields and a weaker dollar improved investor sentiment across the sector.

With geopolitical tensions temporarily easing,marketsare expected to focus increasingly on theFederal Reserve's policy statement, Chair Kevin Warsh's comments and upcoming U.S. economic data. Together, those factors will determine whethergoldcan build on its recent gains or faces renewed pressure from expectations of higher interest rates later this year.
