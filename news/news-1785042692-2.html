Greek gas concession unlocks EU’s Russia sanctions package

Thu, 23 Jul 2026 01:33

BRUSSELS — EU countries reached a deal Thursday on a sweeping 21st sanctions package against Moscow, but only by allowing Greece to keep sending Russian gas to countries outside the bloc.

The final sticking point — a proposed ban on shipping Russian gas outside the EU — was resolved through a limited exemption for contracts signed before Russia’s full-scale invasion of Ukraine, while prohibiting EU operators from entering into new ones, two EU diplomats said, speaking anonymously to discuss the agreement.

The bloc also agreed to freeze its price cap on Russian crude oil for 12 months, the diplomats added. The cap currently stands at $44.10 a barrel and was set to rise because of the war in Iran and an automatic recalculation happening every six months.

Greece had initially resisted the proposed ban on shipping Russian gas to customers outside the bloc, arguing that it went beyond what EU leaders had previously agreed and would be ineffective because vessels could simply re-register abroad.

These sanctions will do little to curb Moscow’s lucrative liquefied natural gas trade, analysts told POLITICO, as an exemption preserves the entirety of the Greek shipping industry’s existing contracts with Russian energy company Novatek, which date back to 2015.

“It is difficult to see how this will significantly disrupt Dynagas’s current Yamal operations or the revenues they help generate for Russia,” said Alexander Kirk, a sanctions campaigner at NGO Urgewald.

The carveout follows pressure from Athens, which warned that an outright ban would hit Greek tanker companies, particularly Dynagas, the shipping group owned by billionaire George Prokopiou. Dynagas handled around 35 percent of exports from Novatek’s Yamal LNG project in the first half of 2026 and operates five of the 15 specialized Arc7 ice-class tankers — special vessels needed to navigate Arctic waters.

A third diplomat said the LNG export exemption will remain in place for one year and renew automatically unless EU capitals agree to end it during a review. Because sanctions decisions require unanimity, withdrawing the exemption could prove difficult.

In return, Greece agreed to a 12-month oil price cap freeze. EU Commission PresidentUrsula von der Leyen celebrated the news on Bluesky, writing that it would prevent “the Russian war machine” from benefiting “from market shocks.”

The package will make it harder for people who served in the Russian armed forces to enter the EU. Although several capitals pushed for a blanket ban, implementation concerns led governments to agree on narrower restrictions.

The deal adds more vessels to Russia’s so-called shadow fleet blacklist, barring them from receiving services or insurance in the EU, and also targets ships that support the fleet. Another 32 Russian banks will be banned from transactions with EU counterparts.

A proposed ban on Russian fish exports was dropped from the final package after pressure from the fishing industry. Companies in several EU countries rely on cheaper Russian cod and pollock to produce fresh and frozen seafood products.
