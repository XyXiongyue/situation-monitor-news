US plans new Iran sanctions, targets banks

Sun, 30 Aug 2026 14:58

The United States is preparing to impose further financial sanctions on Iran, with Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent saying another bank will be targeted this week as Washington intensifies efforts to isolate Tehran economically.

The move comes as US-Iran hostilities flare again following a month-long lull, while Washington seeks international cooperation to cut Iran’s access to global finance.

Bessent told The Associated Press on Sunday that the Trump administration plans to sanction another bank as part of its campaign to increase economic pressure on Iran.

“This is going to be financial violence if we have to,” Bessent said, warning financial institutions that Washington knows which entities are dealing with Iran.

“We are showing people that we know who you are, you know who you are, and this has got to stop,” he added.

Bessent also told Reuters that the Treasury Department is likely to announce new secondary sanctions against Iran on a weekly basis, initially focusing on banks.

“We're starting with the banks, and we're telling the banks it's not okay to have Iranian money and to aid the regime,” Bessent said.

Washington warns banks over Iranian money

The latest sanctions campaign could go beyond simply placing financial institutions on a sanctions list.

Bessent said the United States could ultimately cut institutions off entirely from the dollar-based international financial system if they continue facilitating transactions involving Iran.

“You're going to see a lot more of these every week,” Bessent said. He said Washington wants banks and financial institutions to sever economic links with Tehran or risk being hit with secondary sanctions.

“There can be no leakage,” Bessent said. “You're either with us or you're with the Iranians.”

The Treasury Department launched its new pressure campaign, dubbed Operation Economic Outcast, last week. The initiative is designed to broaden secondary sanctions and restrict Iran’s access to international financial networks and revenue sources.

Bessent said he would use upcoming meetings with G20 finance ministers and central bank governors to press governments and financial institutions to cut economic ties with Iran.

The campaign represents a shift in emphasis for the Trump administration, which had recently signalled that it wanted to rely more heavily on economic pressure rather than military action.

Washington described the new campaign as an economic “D-Day” against Iran, a country that has already endured decades of US sanctions.

US strikes Iran amid renewed hostilities

The economic campaign comes as military tensions between Washington and Tehran have once again intensified.

US forces struck Iranian rocket launchers on Larak Island in the Strait of Hormuz on Sunday, marking the first known American military action against Iran in about a month and ending a period of relative calm.

Iran condemned the attack as deadly and vowed to retaliate.

The renewed confrontation has complicated Washington’s effort to shift the focus toward financial and economic pressure as the war enters its sixth month.

One of the biggest challenges for the new sanctions strategy is China, Iran’s largest trading partner and leading buyer of Iranian oil.

Bessent said he would meet Chinese officials individually at the G20 meetings in Asheville, North Carolina, and discuss Beijing’s continued economic relationship with Tehran.

He said “all options are on the table” when it comes to potentially sanctioning China over its purchases of Iranian oil and other trade with Iran. However, Bessent rejected suggestions that the Trump administration was hesitant to confront Beijing.

He called that assessment “a completely false narrative that the media picked up on.”

Bessent said China and the United States share an interest in reopening the Strait of Hormuz and preventing Iran from developing a nuclear weapon.

US stops short of sanctioning major Egyptian bank

The first official Treasury action under the latest economic pressure campaign came on Friday. The department issued a proposed rule that, if finalised, would cut the United Arab Emirates branches of Banque Misr, Egypt’s second-largest bank, off from the US financial system.

The proposed measure followed allegations of financial links between the UAE branches and Iran.

By stopping short of directly imposing sanctions on the Egyptian parent bank, the Trump administration appeared to be signalling caution about penalising major trading partners that maintain commercial relationships with Iran.

The approach is particularly significant for countries such as China and India, both of which have important trade ties with Tehran.

Bessent says Iran’s oil shipments are declining

Bessent said he did not agree with critics who argue that the US sanctions campaign cannot succeed unless Washington imposes secondary sanctions on Chinese companies. He said most Chinese purchases of Iranian oil had already been curtailed by the US blockade of Iranian ports.

According to Bessent, the amount of Iranian oil currently being stored aboard tankers is also falling. “Problem solved,” he said.

The comments suggest Washington believes its pressure on Iranian oil exports is already limiting one of Tehran’s most important sources of foreign revenue.

Bessent’s comments came ahead of a meeting of G20 finance leaders in Asheville, North Carolina. He is expected to hold individual discussions with finance ministers and central bank governors from major and developing economies.

The US Treasury secretary intends to encourage governments to cooperate with Washington’s efforts to restrict Iran’s financial networks and economic access.

The campaign could put additional pressure on countries and financial institutions that continue trading with Tehran, particularly if Washington follows through on its warning of weekly secondary sanctions.

While the Trump administration has increasingly emphasised economic measures, the renewed US-Iran military exchange shows that military options remain part of the broader strategy.

Bessent said all options remained on the table regarding Iran and potential pressure on its trading partners.

For now, Washington is preparing a new wave of bank-focused sanctions while seeking support from G20 economies and warning financial institutions that continued dealings with Iran could bring severe consequences.

The combination of renewed military tensions, restrictions on Iranian oil and expanding financial sanctions is likely to keep pressure on Tehran as the six-month-old conflict enters another uncertain phase.
