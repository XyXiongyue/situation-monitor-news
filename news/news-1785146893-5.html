CMA CGM announces emergency fuel surcharge as Middle East crisis escalates

2026-07-27 18:08

inInternational Shipping News27/07/2026

Global container shipping major CMA CGM will implement an emergency fuel surcharge (EFS) on 1 August because of rising hostilities between the US and Iran in the Middle East.

The EFS – on all global trade lanes – comes as energy prices are surging following the renewed escalation of hostilities in the Strait of Hormuz and rising threats of Houthi attacks in the Bab el-Mandeb Strait.

The EFS is $150/TEU (20-foot equivalent unit) on all long-distance trips in the head haul and $75/TEU on all backhauls as well as regional services.

According to the United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO), which operates under the UK Royal Navy, Yemen-based Houthis attacked a tanker in the Red Sea for the first time since September.Lars Jensen, president of consultancy firm Vespucci Maritime, said there is still a steady stream of traffic through the Bab el-Mandeb Strait but that the latest attacks are a clear worsening of the maritime situation in the region.

“For now, the regional carriers appear to continue to operate, but it would be very problematic for Asia-Red Sea cargo flows if this was to be impeded,” Jensen said. “We should hope this is not going to be the case, but just in case, shippers might want to be prepared that if Bab el-Mandeb genuinely is closed off, cargo already on vessels from Asia to the Red Sea is likely to suddenly be discharged in the ‘wrong’ ports across Asia, similar to what we saw when Hormuz was initially closed.”

The rising hostilities in the Middle East pushed Brent crude prices above $100/barrel. Brent was just above $70/barrel at the beginning of the month.

BACKGROUNDTransits through the Strait of Hormuz resumed on 19 June after the US and Iran signed a memorandum of understanding (MoU). Traffic through the Strait had been essentially halted since late February when the US and Israel attacked Iran.

Traffic through the Strait of Hormuz is again limited.

According to the Strait of Hormuz tracker, a free, real-time dashboard that tracks the ongoing crisis using AI-powered analysis of current Strait conditions, insurance markets and diplomatic developments using real-time web data and AIS data for vessel positions, 15 vessels have transited the Strait over the past 24 hours.

Prior to 28 February, traffic through the waterway was around 150 vessels/day.

The closure has had significant impact on crude oil and chemical markets as around one-third of global seaborne crude flows and up to 20% of the world’s total oil flows pass through.

The closure had less impact on container shipping as less than 2% of global container capacity passes through the Strait each year, though it contributed to higher rates mostly because of surging bunker fuel prices.

Container ships and costs for shipping containers are relevant to the chemical industry because while most chemicals are liquids and are shipped in tankers, container ships transport polymers, such as polyethylene (PE) and polypropylene (PP), which are shipped in pellets. Titanium dioxide (TiO2) is also shipped in containers.
