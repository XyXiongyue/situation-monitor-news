Houthis strike Saudi oil tanker as Red Sea risks escalate

Wed, 29 Jul 2026 04:13

Fresh concerns over global oil supplies resurfaced on Wednesday after Yemen’s Iran-backed Houthi movement claimed responsibility for a missile attack on a Saudi oil tanker in the Red Sea, highlighting the growing threat to one of the world’s key energy shipping corridors.

The Houthis said the Saudi-flagged oil tanker NCC GHAZAL was targeted after allegedly violating the group’s maritime blockade on Saudi shipping through the Red Sea and the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, which was announced last week.

“The Yemeni armed forces carried out a military operation targeting the Saudi oil tanker (NCC GHAZAL) for violating the maritime navigation ban imposed on the Saudi enemy and for refusing warning calls, using a number of ballistic missiles,” Houthi military spokesperson Yahya Saree said in a statement posted on X.

According to the Houthis, the tanker was forced to retreat after the attack.

The latest strike comes as shipping companies continue to reassess security risks in the Red Sea despite a recent easing of direct hostilities between the United States and Iran.

Maritime intelligence firm Windward said the NCC GHAZAL had displayed what it described as “high-risk” operating behaviour in the weeks leading up to the attack, including a seven-day Automatic Identification System (AIS) blackout off Yemen’s coast, multiple ship-to-ship transfers and repeated destination changes.

The vessel reportedly switched off its transponder again on July 23 and has not transmitted its location since, according to Windward.

The attack coincided with a broader escalation across the Middle East.

The U.S. Central Command (CENTCOM) said Iranian forces launched multiple ballistic missiles at American military bases across the region in what it described as a surprise attack.

CENTCOM also announced that U.S. and Saudi forces later carried out precision strikes against what it called terrorist logistics and weapons facilities in eastern Iraq.

The renewed security concerns are expected to further weigh on tanker traffic through the Red Sea, even as tensions between Washington and Tehran have moderated in recent days.

Saudi Arabia has already begun adjusting its export logistics to reduce exposure to the Bab el-Mandeb Strait.

Shipping data show that more than half a dozen empty supertankers are currently heading to Egypt’s Sidi Kerir terminal to load Saudi crude, allowing exports to bypass the increasingly risky southern Red Sea route.

The kingdom has also stepped up shipments from its Yanbu export terminal to Ain Sukhna on Egypt’s Red Sea coast, before transporting crude through the SUMED pipeline to Sidi Kerir on the Mediterranean for onward export.

The rerouting strategy reflects growing concerns among producers and shipowners that persistent attacks around the Bab el-Mandeb Strait could further disrupt global oil flows, increase freight costs and sustain geopolitical risk premiums in crude markets.
