Ship Recycling Still Subdued

2026-07-30 05:13

inHellenic Shipping News30/07/2026

Best Oasis added that “the Bangladeshi ship recycling market remained firm during the week, with buyers continuing to show strong interest in acquiring additional vessels. Local market activity remained relatively subdued; however, buyer appetite for recycling candidates continued to be healthy. Prices remained firm, supported by sustained purchasing interest despite limited momentum in the local market. The Pakistani ship recycling market remained buoyant during the week, with buyers actively seeking suitable recycling candidates. Mid-sized vessels in the 7,000–10,000 LDT range continue to be the most preferred among local buyers. While Pakistani buyers remain unable to match the pricing levels offered by Bangladesh, market sentiment remains optimistic. Confidence is supported by limited steel availability, as reduced supplies from Iran are expected to provide support to the local recycling market. However, concerns are growing over the broader economic outlook, as a sharp increase in fuel prices over the past 8–10 days is expected to fuel inflation and could weigh on market sentiment in the near term. Finally, in Turkiye, the Turkish recycling market recorded a modest positive correction, with both imported and local steel prices increasing by approximately USD 4–5 per tonne. Despite firmer steel values, vessel prices remained unchanged as buyers continued to maintain a cautious approach. Overall sentiment improved slightly, although the increase in steel prices has not yet translated into stronger vessel pricing”, Best Oasis concluded.

Meanwhile, in a separate report this week, shipbroker Intermodal commented that “at Alang, the market was muted last week amid tepid demand, while rising energy prices and the weakening of the Indian Rupee against the US Dollar weighed on sentiment. However, the backdrop in the steel market is supportive, with steel plate prices rising, driven mainly by tightening supply, as steel processing operations are curtailed by the monsoons. Finally, on the policy front, the proposed listing of two Alang facilities is a positive signal towards the opening of a first approved channel for EU flagged recycling candidates to be directed to the Indian market. Bangladesh is emerging from a period of deadly monsoon flooding impacting economic activity, with the ship recycling segment recovering in parallel, as operations at yards and steel mills resume operations.

Source: Intermodal Shipbrokers

Despite limited activity, buying interest remains healthy, particularly for bulk carriers, although the recent case of a tanker sanctioned after clearance but before beaching has made buyers more reluctant and increased demand for compliance-clear tonnage. Overall sentiment is positive, supported by expectations that candidates delayed during the monsoon period will be offered to the post-monsoon market. Conditions at Gadani hub remained broadly unchanged, defined by a limited pool of candidates and healthy buying appetite. The steel market is supportive, with prices firming on the back of supply constraints, as monsoon-related adverse weather continues to disrupt mills’ operations. There is optimism that government tax relief on property transactions and support for affordable housing will boost construction activity, and in turn steel demand, once the monsoon passes. Meanwhile, news of the country’s credit rating upgrade, on the back of foreign capital inflows, added to the positive sentiment. In Turkey, despite improving conditions in the steel market, activity remains flat as buyers maintain a wait-and-see stance amid a limited number of candidates and further weakening of the local currency”, Intermodal concluded.Nikos Roussanoglou, Hellenic Shipping News Worldwide
