OPEC+ May Pause Oil Output Increases After September

OPEC+ is considering pausing its recent production quota increases after September, signaling that months of higher output targets may soon come to an end despite continued supply disruptions in the global oil market.

According to Reuters, citing sources familiar with the discussions, the producer alliance is considering leaving production quotas unchanged from October 2026 through January 2027, when a new quota system is scheduled to take effect.

The proposal comes ahead of the group's August 2 meeting, where members are expected to set production targets for September. Reuters reported on July 23 that OPEC+ is likely to approve another increase of 188,000 barrels per day, marking the fourth consecutive monthly rise in production quotas.

That increase would complete the reversal of the group's voluntary 1.65 million-barrel-per-day production cut introduced in 2023 and later adjusted following the United Arab Emirates' exit from the alliance in May. Since April, Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Algeria, Kazakhstan and Oman have collectively restored about 1.15 million barrels per day of production quotas, according to Reuters calculations.

Despite the successive quota increases, actual OPEC+ production has risen far more slowly. Official OPEC data show the alliance produced 36.28 million barrels per day in June, nearly 7 million barrels per day below pre-war levels, as several members remain unable to increase exports.

Iraq continues to face export infrastructure bottlenecks, while Kazakhstan reduced production after drone attacks damaged the Caspian Pipeline Consortium terminal on the Black Sea.

Russia has also seen repeated Ukrainian drone strikes disrupt refinery operations. Saudi Arabia still has spare production capacity but faces growing risks to its own export infrastructure. As a result, higher production quotas have so far had only a limited impact on actual global supply.

Brent crude briefly approached $100 a barrel on July 24 as renewed threats to Red Sea exports heightened supply concerns before retreating to around $90 on July 28 after the United States and Iran suspended their exchanges of strikes, according to Trading Economics.

Analysts at Goldman Sachs, cited by OilPrice, say OPEC+'s strategy is intended primarily to reassure markets that the alliance stands ready to increase supply once geopolitical conditions allow. Meanwhile, the International Energy Agency (IEA) said in its July 2026 market report that global oil production remains 9.4 million barrels per day below pre-war levels.
