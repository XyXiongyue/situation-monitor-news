US outlines broader sanctions drive targeting Iran and its trading networks

Tue, 25 Aug 2026 01:48

The United States has announced a wider sanctions campaign against Iran, extending pressure to trading partners and sectors including shipping, technology and digital assets. The measures also target nearly 60 entities, individuals and vessels.

WASHINGTON: US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent announced on Monday what the Treasury Department described as an economic onslaught against Iran, combining fresh sanctions with warnings for countries and businesses that continue commercial activity with Tehran.

According to the measures outlined by Bessent, Washington will set deadlines for other governments to wind down economic engagement with Iran and has threatened unspecified action if they fail to do so. His statement, however, did not include some of the harshest possible penalties and did not identify which countries could be targeted or when any such steps would take effect.

The new approach also broadens the scope of activity that could trigger secondary sanctions on countries, individuals or companies regardless of location. The Treasury said foreign entities involved in money laundering or sanctions evasion for Iran could be cut off from the US financial system.

Under the announcement, additional categories of trade or support linked to Iran may now expose outside parties to US penalties. These areas include digital assets such as cryptocurrency, technology, gold, aviation and shipping.

The Treasury Department's Office of Foreign Assets Control, or OFAC, has also imposed sanctions on nearly 60 entities, individuals and vessels that it said were helping Iranian activity. The sanctions package targets what the US describes as the Iranian defence ministry's ballistic and nuclear procurement network, a cyber group accused by Washington of attacks on US infrastructure, and shipping, trading and financial networks tied to Iranian oil sales.

OFAC also suspended several licences that had previously permitted some remittance payments to Iran and some Iranian access to US cultural and academic systems.

Measures aimed at procurement, cyber activity and oil exports

More than 20 of the new sanctions focus on entities and individuals in the Middle East and Asia that the Treasury said were providing financial and logistical backing for Iranian nuclear research and missile development. Among them are companies based in China that, according to the Treasury, supplied dual-use items to an Iranian technology institute that had already been sanctioned.

The United States is also widening sanctions on several people who had already been designated earlier, accusing them of involvement in cybercrime or activities that threaten US national security.

In the energy sector, the latest steps are intended to curb Iranian oil exports by targeting vessel brokers, bunkering service providers and financial intermediaries. The measures name multiple individuals and entities in the United Arab Emirates, as well as others in Singapore and Hong Kong.

The Treasury further identified five tankers as part of what it called a shadow fleet and designated them as blocked property. Under that status, OFAC bars US nationals and people based in the United States from conducting financial transactions involving those vessels.
