Oil Prices Fall as Iran Negotiation Hopes Return

Aug 25, 2026 at 10:53 | Tom Kool

U.S. sanctions pressure on Iran intensifies, but Pakistani mediation hopes push Brent back to $89.

US Blockade Leaves Iran’s Oil Trade on Borrowed Time

-The announcement of the Trump administration’s ‘Economic D-Day’ sanctions on Iran coincides with an overall slump in Tehran’s crude exports,chokedoff by the US Navy’s maritime blockade in the Gulf of Oman.

-Iran’s August crude exports have so far been minimalcompared to the 2025 average of 1.7 million b/d, averaging only slightly around 0.3 million b/d with only one confirmed loading at the country’s largest terminal in Kharg Island.

- Thanks to the volumes evacuated during the three-week period of free navigation in June-July,Chinese imports of Iranian barrels are slightly above Tehran’s pace of loading,around 800,000 b/d, however due to drawing stocks they are set to decline further next month.

- Should the US maritime blockade of Iran continue, China could run out of Iranian options by October, reflected in soaring Iranian differentials as Iran Light is nowtradedat a $4 per barrel premium to ICE Brent.

-Iranian floating storage outside of the Gulf has shrunk to just 24 million barrels, down 7 million barrels since the beginning of August, with Chinese buyers mopping up the last remaining volumes.

-US shale pioneerContinental Resourceshasagreedto acquire privately held Firebird Energy II, boosting its Permian portfolio by 54,000 net acres that currently yield some 32,000 boe/d of oil-weighted output.

- Brazil’s state oil firmPetrobras(NYSE:PBR) hasentereddirect negotiations with the government of Ghana for exploration contracts covering 4 offshore blocks in the underexplored Keta basin, seeking to expand beyond Brazil.

- US oil majorChevron(NYSE:CVX) hasfinalizedits return to Libya, signing a production-sharing contract covering onshore block S4 in the Sirte basin, containing one 100-million-boe gas discovery and several unconfirmed prospects.

- Guyana’s regulatory body has vowed to investigate a fire that broke out this weekend atExxonMobil’s(NYSE:XOM) Liza Unity floating production vessel,leadingto a suspension in operations.

- The $8 billion sale ofShell’s(LON:SHEL) US chemical assets has drawninterestfrom several high-profile buyers, with ExxonMobil and LyondellBasell expressing interest along with Kuwait’s state oil firm KPC.

Tuesday, August 25, 2026

The Trump administration is ratcheting up sanctions pressure on Iran, threatening to sanction all commercial partners of Tehran in case they don’t wind down trade (without specifying the timelines for doing so). At the same time, Pakistan’s mediation efforts have offset the bullish momentum following Scott Bessent’s Monday press conference, with oil market participants still hoping for some form of negotiated settlement, sending ICE Brent prices lower to $89 per barrel.

Washington Takes Its Iran Sanctions War Global.US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessentunveiledthe Trump administration’s long-mooted “economic D-Day” measures against Tehran, threatening Iran’s main trading partners, without hinting at China, with exclusion from the dollar system.

Iran Turns Its Hormuz Blacklist into a Contagion Risk.Tehran has threatened fines, detention and cargoconfiscationagainst 45 tankers deemed non-compliant for dark transits through the Strait of Hormuz, warning any vessel conducting ship-to-ship transfers with them could also be listed.

Hormuz Traffic Collapses as Iran Widens Its Shipping Threat. Only 2 commodity vesselscrossedthe Hormuz strait on Monday, a notable plunge compared to the previous 10-day average of 14, following the Iranian authorities’ threats against dark transits and vessels deemed non-compliant.Saudi Arabia’s Hormuz Bypass Runs into Red Sea Risk.A Houthi attack on the Amzan tanker owned by Saudi Arabia’s national shipping firm Bahri near Riyadh’s only operational loading port of Yanbu exposed the vulnerability of the East-West pipeline,forcingall tankers to move towards the Suez.

IEA Keeps Its Oil Reserve Firepower in Check.The International Energy Agency is notdiscussinganother emergency release after deploying 400 million barrels in March, according to its chief Fatih Birol, claiming to monitor the current situation as 80% of the IEA’s strategic stocks remain intact.

Ukraine’s Drones Keep Russian Diesel Off the Market.According to Russia’s deputy prime minister Alexander Novak, Moscow mayextendits diesel export ban through October after a series of Ukrainian drone strikes on southern refineries perpetuated the pressure on domestic fuel balances.

Trump Rebuilds His China Tariff Wall.US President Donald Trump is set toimposean additional 7.5% tariff on Chinese goods over allegations of excess manufacturing capacity, restoring the White House’s second-term duties to 20%, less than a month before the September 24 Trump-Xi Summit.

Britain Eyes an LNG Lifeline as North Sea Gas Fades.The UK government isreportedlyconsidering investment in LNG import capacity amidst declining output, defying calls for a reversal in London’s repressive oil and gas policy that slapped a 78% headline tax under the Energy Profits Levy.

Qatar’s LNG Woes Shift from Gas to Ships. QatarEnergy’s 77 mtpa Ras Laffanliquefactionplant is running short of available gas carriers as the Middle Eastern country’s Hormuz transits have stalled completely, leaving 1.2 million mt LNG afloat in the Gulf and raising the prospect of production cuts.

Iran Finds More Gas, but Not Yet More Supply.Iran hasreportedthe discovery of a giant gas field containing up to 5.7 TCf of recoverable gas, the 2ndsignificant gas find in the southern province of Fars alongside Pazan, with Tehran aiming for a commissioning date in the second half of 2029.

EPA Relief Threat Deflates US Biofuel Credits.Prices for US ethanol blending creditsplunged16% to $1.75 after the Environmental Protection Agency delayed its compliance deadline and pending refinery exemptions raised expectations that 1.2–1.8 billion credits could return to the market.

Braskem’s Petchem Slump Ends in Restructuring.Latin America’s largest petrochemical producer Braskem (NYSE:BAK) willrenegotiate$10.9 billion of its unsecured debt after weak industry margins and a salt-mine disaster drained liquidity, potentially opening the door to new investors.

Pemex Battles Fire at Pajaritos Terminal.Mexico's state oil firm Pemex said a fire at the Pajaritos storage terminal remains under control but has yet to be fully extinguished, claiming the blaze wassparkedby an electrical storm, derailing crude loadings for the 100,000 b/d Isthmus crude stream.

Trump Wins a Procedural Round on Offshore Drilling.An Alaska judgedismisseda lawsuit from environmentalists challenging President Trump’s move to allow offshore drilling, however left the question of whether a president can legally reverse a predecessor’s coastal protections unresolved.

China Has the Zinc, the LME Has the Squeeze.Zinc prices hit a four-year high of $3,825 per tonne as LME inventories fell 25% since June, driven bysoaringChinese stocks and record-negative treatment charges confirming concentrate scarcity, with the LME cash premium jumping to $132 per tonne.
