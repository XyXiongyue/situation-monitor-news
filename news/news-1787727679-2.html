US-Iran War Live Updates: Global economy weathered Hormuz oil shock ‘better than feared’, says IMF chief

The global economy has absorbed the energy shock from the Strait of Hormuz closure better than expected, helped by withdrawals from oil and gas reserves, higher non-Gulf supplies and an AI investment boom, IMF Managing Director Kristalina Georgieva has said.

“The energy shock is not over,” Georgieva warned, noting that reserves were shrinking and the northern hemisphere winter was approaching. She said another rise in oil prices could push up inflation, keep interest rates elevated and weigh on debt servicing and economic activity.

Georgieva described the outlook as a tug-of-war between the supply shock from the West Asia war and the demand boost from AI investment.

US-Iran War Live Updates: China emerges as a major test for Trump’s Iran sanctions campaign

The Trump administration’s sanctions against Iran may be constrained by its effort to preserve a trade truce with China ahead of President Xi Jinping’s expected Washington visit next month.

China is Iran’s largest trading partner and leading oil buyer, but Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent offered few details on how Beijing would be targeted under the new campaign.

“The announcement yesterday was very careful in my view to avoid specifics (against China), which could have led to a disruption in the summit,” Edgard Kagan of the Center for Strategic and International Studies told AP.

US-Iran War Live Updates: Bessent discusses Iran sanctions campaign with Bahrain finance minister

US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent spoke with Bahrain’s Finance Minister Shaikh Salman bin Khalifa Al Khalifa about “Operation Economic Outcast”, the Treasury Department said.
