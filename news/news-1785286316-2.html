Houthis Claim Missile Attack on Saudi Oil Tanker in Red Sea Escalation

Tue, 28 Jul 2026 17:42

Yemen's Iran-backed Houthi movement says it has targeted a Saudi oil tanker with missiles in the Red Sea, in what would be the latest escalation in regional tensions linked to the conflict involving Iran, the United States and its allies. Saudi authorities have not confirmed the reported attack on the Saudi-flagged tanker NCC Ghazal, which public maritime tracking services show was last located along Saudi Arabia's Red Sea coast five days ago.

In a statement, the Houthis said the vessel had ignored repeated warnings and had been forced to reverse course. The group did not say whether the tanker had been struck or specify its location at the time of the incident.

Hours after the Houthi announcement, the United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO), a monitoring organisation operated by the British navy, said a tanker transiting the Red Sea had reported hearing an explosion. It said all crew members were safe.

If confirmed, it would be at least thefourth oil tanker targetedsince the Houthis announced earlier this month that they would seek to blockade shipping to and from Saudi Arabia in the Red Sea.

Shortly after the Houthi claim, Saudi Arabia's defence ministry said it had intercepted and destroyed several drones launched for a second consecutive day by Iran-aligned militias in Iraq that were aimed at Saudi oil facilities.

Since announcing the blockade, both sides have accused each other of carrying out attacks. A Saudi-led military coalition has also resumed air strikes in Houthi-controlled areas of Yemen for the first time in several years.

Separately, the Houthis said on Monday they had targeted crude oil supplies and transport routes linking Saudi Arabia's eastern oil fields with the Red Sea export hub of Yanbu. Saudi authorities have not commented publicly on that claim.

The latest developments come as shipping routes across the Middle East remain under increasing strain.

Since March, Iran has effectively blockaded the Strait of Hormuz, forcing Saudi Arabia to rely more heavily on exporting oil through Red Sea ports.

The Houthis control much of Yemen's Red Sea coastline, including territory near the Bab al-Mandab Strait, one of the world's busiest maritime chokepoints linking the Red Sea to the Indian Ocean. Any sustained disruption there could affect shipping routes to Asian markets and force vessels onto significantly longer journeys.

Last week, the Houthis claimed responsibility for attacks on two tankers in the Red Sea. Saudi Arabia confirmed one of those incidents and later said a Saudi-owned vessel had suffered hull damage after being targeted on Friday.

The Houthis, who form part of what Iran describes as its "axis of resistance" alongside armed groups in Iraq, Hamas in Gaza and Hezbollah in Lebanon, have controlled large parts of Yemen following years of conflict with the Saudi-backed internationally recognised government.

Although a ceasefire has largely held since 2022, tensions have risen sharply this month after the Houthis accused Saudi Arabia of attacking Sanaa International Airport during a dispute involving an Iranian aircraft. The group later claimed responsibility for an attack on Abha airport in southern Saudi Arabia, marking its first direct strike on Saudi territory since early 2022.

The Houthis have previously targeted commercial shipping in the Red Sea using drones, missiles and hijackings, particularly during the war in Gaza, saying they were acting in support of Palestinians.

The latest claims come as maritime traffic through the Strait of Hormuz has also fallen sharply following the conflict that began after US and Israeli strikes on Iran on 28 February, with many shipping companies avoiding the route because of security concerns.
