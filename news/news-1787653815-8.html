State Department offers $10 million reward for information on Iranian IRGC leaders

The U.S. State Department said Monday that it was offering rewards of up to $10 million for information on key leaders of Iran's powerful Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC), according to the department'swebsite.

The agency'sRewards for Justice program identifies five senior Iranian commanderswho it accuses of targeting U.S. personnel with armed attacks and U.S. infrastructure with cyber operations.

The State Department says the individuals command or direct various elements of the IRGC and also accuses the force of planning, organizing and carrying out terrorism worldwide. The IRGC has beendesignated a Foreign Terrorist Organizationby the U.S. government for almost a decade.

Price of oil falls after U.S. sanctions announcement

The price of Brent crude, the international standard for oil, fell 0.6% early Tuesday to $90.04 per barrel. U.S. benchmark crude oil shed 0.6% to trade at $84.51 per barrel.

Last month, the price of Brent zigzagged between $72 and $102 as hopes rose and fell that the United States and Iran could reach a deal that would allow oil tankers to freely exit the Persian Gulf again.

The new U.S. sanctions announced Monday helped drag the value of Iran's currency, the rial, to a record low against the U.S. dollar.

While the key oil prices have dipped again slightly this week, they remain up about 35% on the year, compared to pre-war levels, and about 2% higher than they were a month ago.

China vows to defend its "rights and interests" in face of U.S. sanctions on Iran

China has defended its cooperation with Iran on Tuesday when asked about the new U.S. sanctions on Tehran, which are aimed at pressuring the country's allies

A foreign ministry spokesperson said China will "firmly safeguard its own rights and interests," when asked about the new sanctions announced by Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent Monday.

The spokesperson added that China's cooperation with Iran should not be interfered with or disrupted.

"China has already stated many times that it firmly opposes illegal unilateral sanctions ... China will take all necessary measures to firmly safeguard its own rights and interests," foreign ministry spokesman Lin Jian told a regular news briefing.

Pakistan says talks with Iran yielded "significant progress"

Pakistan's interior minister heralded "significant progress" after a meeting with Iran's president on Tuesday that focused on the U.S.-Iran war, according to the Reuters news agency.

Talks between Pakistan and Iran were held on Tuesday and focused on measures to prevent further escalation and the Strait of Hormuz, according to a statement from the Pakistani military quoted by Reuters.

Pakistan was a key mediator in the memorandum of understanding signed by Washington and Tehran in June. It set an ambitious 60-day deadline for ending the war with Iran and reaching an accord on its nuclear program. That period ended last week, after weeks of stalled progress and flare-ups, with both sides declaring they had abandoned it.

Projectile hits oil tanker in Strait of Hormuz off Oman coast, U.K. maritime agency says

An oil tanker was struck and disabled by a projectile in the Strait of Hormuz off the coast of Oman, causing damage but no casualties, a British maritime agency said.

"The Master of an oil tanker reports the vessel has been struck by an unknown projectile causing damage to the engine room and disabling the vessel," with the crew reported as safe, the United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations said.

The incident occurred 9 nautical miles northeast of Ash Shishah in Oman, according to the maritime agency.

The online statement did not specify the vessel's nationality or direction of travel.

Tehran has imposed an effective blockade of the strait since the United States and Israel struck Iran in late February, and wants to charge tolls for passage, attacking ships it accuses of attempting to circumvent its preferred route.

Iran predicts "another defeat" for U.S. with new sanctions

Iranian Economy Minister Ali Madanizadeh predicted on Monday "another defeat" for the U.S., saying the Islamic Republic had a "two-year plan" to counter Washington'snew sanctions.

"They have done everything they could to test the determination of the Iranian people and the country's officials, but they have failed every time. It seems they wished to suffer yet another defeat," Madanizadeh told state television.

"We've been waiting for these plans for a long time, and the government is and was ready and has a two-year plan to manage these events," he added.

Netanyahu says Iran tried to kill one of his sons

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said Monday that Iran had attempted to assassinate one of his two sons, without offering further details.

"Something incredible happened: Iran targeted one of my sons. Iran tried to kill one of my sons," Netanyahu said during a phone interview with Israel's Channel 14.

The claim came during a discussion about reports that the Shin Bet domestic security agency had declined to provide a security detail for Gadi Eisenkot, the former army chief who is running neck and neck with Netanyahu ahead of elections on Oct. 27.

Netanyahu voiced support for around-the-clock protection for Eisenkot, saying it was "not a luxury."

"Without that, (the Iranians) will succeed," he said.

Netanyahu has two sons and a daughter. It remains unclear which of his sons was targeted.

71 commercial ships redirected in U.S. naval blockade against Iran, Central Command says

The U.S. military has redirected 71 commercial ships in its blockade against Iran, U.S. Central Command said Monday.

The new numberposted by Central Commandmeans U.S. forces have redirected seven commercial ships since last Monday, when the totalwas 64. Over the course of the blockade, the U.S. has also disabled three ships and boarded two to enforce compliance.

Central Command said Monday that over 40 ships supporting humanitarian aid have been allowed to pass through the blockade.

Iran-linked hackers took U.K. power plant offline for first time, reports say

Iranian-affiliated hackers successfully but temporarily took a power plant offline in the United Kingdom, according to multiple British media outlets.

The Telegraph and Financial Times reported the attack, which they say happened in July but is only now being reported, is believed to be the first time Iranian-linked hackers managed to shut down such a facility in the country.

Of further concern, the breach also happened the same month Iranian-linked hackers are believed to have targeted the water systemsof a dozen states including New Jersey, Minnesota, Georgia and South Dakota,locking out operators and causing pressure loss and flooding.

CBS News reached out to the U.K.'s cybersecurity agency, the National Cyber Security Centre, but it neither confirmed nor denied any hacking had occurred.

Iran war won't end with more U.S. economic pressure, China says

Before the U.S. announced new sanctions on countries that trade with Iran, China criticized the move.

"They will only exacerbate tensions and escalate the situation, which is in no one's interest," Foreign Ministry spokesperson Lin Jian said in Beijing.

China is calling on all parties to avoid measures "that could further intensify differences and conflicts and disrupt global economic development and financial stability," he added.

China is considered the biggest buyer of Iranian oil, and was importing more than 80% of Iranian oil before the U.S.-Israeli war with Iran.

Bessent says entities laundering money for Iran will be removed from U.S. dollar system

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said any entity that facilitates money laundering on behalf of Iran "will be removed from the U.S. dollar system. The clock just started ticking."

The new sanctions announced Monday expand the categories for secondary sanctions on entities and countries that transact with Tehran and also introduce new sanctions across a variety of vital sectors, including digital assets, gold, aviation, technology and shipping.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent is holding a press conference at the Treasury Department announcing Operation Economic Outcast, the Trump administration's ramped up push to use economic tools to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and end the war with Iran.

"Today, at President Trump's direction, the United States Treasury has begun Operation Economic Outcast, an unprecedented campaign against the Islamic Republic of Iran and its enablers," Bessent said.

Trump spoke with Pakistani Army chief before visit to Tehran, as Pakistan pushes for renewed talks

President Trump spoke with Pakistani Army chief Asim Munir ahead of Munir's trip to Tehran on Monday, a source familiar with the matter told CBS News on Monday.

Iran's foreign ministry spokesman Esmail Baghaei announced Sunday that Munir would visit Tehran as the head of an official Pakistani delegation. He said the visit was in line with strengthening Iran-Pakistan bilateral cooperation and continuing Pakistan's efforts to help strengthen peace and security in the region.

Pakistan has served as a key mediator between the U.S. and Iran, and Munir is thought to have developed a personal rapport with Mr. Trump.

Pakistani officials said his visit to the Iranian capital was part of the country's efforts "to promote regional peace and stability."

The Reuters news agency quoted sources as saying Munir was expected to try and draw Iran back into a dialogue with the U.S., after President Trump said at least twice last week that all talks had ended.

Houthis claim missile attack on Saudi tanker in Red Sea

Houthi military spokesman Brig. Gen. Yahya Saree said in a video statement Monday that the Iranian-allied rebels had struck the Saudi oil tanker Amzan in the northern Red Sea, off the coast of Yanbu, with a ballistic missile.

The claim came hours after the British Navy's Maritime Operations Center said a vessel was struck by an unknown projectile about 63 miles off Yanbu, causing a fire on the main deck, but harming no members of the crew.

Saree said the Houthis "succeeded in targeting the Saudi enemy's oil tanker Amzan," calling the strike "accurate and direct" and claiming it had prompted "a number of other ships in the targeted area to flee."

He said it was part of the Iranian-allied group's ongoing effort to blockade Saudi ships and ports in the Red Sea, which the Houthis announced in July. They have sinceattacked multiple Saudi-owned tankersin the region.

Iran had warned throughout the first phases of the war with the U.S. that it could ask its Houthi allies to disrupt shipping through the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, which is the southern gateway to the Red Sea, in response to U.S. naval operations in the Strait of Hormuz and Persian Gulf.

Iranian police chief says U.S. economic warfare intended to foment nationwide unrest

Iran's national police chief, Brig. Gen. Ahmad-Reza Radan, warned Monday that Iran's adversaries were seeking to exploit economic and social grievances to trigger unrest, potentially across the entire country.

"The enemy is seeking to create unrest in a part of the country or throughout the country, and there are many pretexts, including livelihoods, gasoline, the economy and unemployment," Radan said hours before the U.S. Treasury unveiled a new package of sanctions that President Trump has said will hit Iran with an "economic D-Day."

Radan, cited by the semi-official Tasnim News agency, urged police to adopt a preemptive approach to any potential unrest, including by identifying what he described as hidden networks in society and on social media and confronting those who seek to incite the public.

Trump says Iran is "completely collapsing"

Hours before the U.S. Treasury was set to unveil a raft of new sanctions aimed at squeezing Iran's economy to force the Islamic Republic to capitulate in the war launched by the U.S. and Israel almost six months ago, President Trump declared the theocracy to be "completely collapsing."

In a briefpost on his Truth Socialnetwork Monday, Mr. Trump wrote simply that "IRAN IS COMPLETELY COLLAPSING!!!" without offering any further context.

He previously vowed that the new U.S. tactic in the war, which represents a shift from military strikes and threats to sanctions-based financial warfare, would impose an "economic D-Day" on Iran.

Iran has, thus far, dismissed the U.S. plans to ratchet up sanctions, noting that the Islamic Republic has already withstood decades of U.S. economic punishment.

Iran's central bank chief says U.S. has long wielded sanctions, and "they were unable to do anything"

Iran's Central Bank Governor Abdolnaser Hemmati said Monday that the country's leaders must speak honestly with the public about economic difficulties facing the country, but he argued that pressure from the U.S. will ultimately fail to force the nation to capitulate.

"I believe we must speak honestly with the people," Hemmati said at the Entrepreneurs' Assembly in Tehran. "We are meeting the people's basic needs. If our exports are cut off, we have made preparations for that as well."

Hemmati compared the current situation, as the Trump administration prepares to impose an "economic D-Day" on Iran, with the previous "maximum pressure" campaign adopted by Mr. Trump during his first term in office.

"I was also the head of the Central Bank from 2018 to 2020. Our circumstances were almost exactly like this. They imposed maximum pressure, imposed secondary sanctions, sanctioned all our banks, sanctioned all our agriculture, sanctioned all our oil purchases — they sanctioned everything," he said. "The country stood and got through that period. It is the same now. We stood through those conditions and, in practice, they were unable to do anything."

Hemmati questioned what additional measures Washington could even impose after having already subjected Iran to extensive sanctions for many years, and he argued that the U.S. has a greater interest than Iran in bringing the conflict to an end, and quickly.

"It won't take very long," Hemmati said. "The information I have tells me that it will not last long. They need this matter to be resolved sooner than we do."

Iran will respond to U.S. sanctions, foreign ministry says

Iran's foreign ministry spokesman warned Monday that Tehran would respond harshly to new U.S. sanctions, including with measures against countries it sees as cooperating with Washington.

"Any escalation of this situation will undoubtedly bring about consequences," Esmaeil Baghaei said. "Our hands are not tied."

Iran downplays potential impact of new sanctions

Iran's foreign ministry spokesman downplayed the potential impact of new economic sanctions the U.S. is set to unveil on Monday.

Asked by a reporter about their impact, spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei said previous similar attempts by the U.S. to pressure Iran had failed, and "the Iranian people will show that they are determined and steadfast in safeguarding their rights."

Baghaei said the new measures were being imposed because the Trump administration had "miscalculated" its attacks and believed "military aggression could bring the Iranians to their knees."

South Korea says U.S. canceled military exercise over Iran war

The South Korean military said Monday the U.S. canceled a large, joint military exercise that had been scheduled to take place next month due to force limitations amid the war with Iran.

The Ssang Yong (Twin Dragons) exercise is a joint amphibious landing drill conducted regularly since 2012 by the South Korean Navy and Marine Corps alongside the U.S. Navy and Marine Corps. British and other allied forces have taken part in previous years.

A spokesperson for the South Korean Marines said its U.S. counterparts had notified the force in June that "due to the situation in the Middle East," the U.S.'s ability to deploy forces for the exercise was constrained this year.

The news of this year's Ssang Yong drills being called off came a week after the U.S. cut short another major joint military exercise with South Korea,on President Trump's orders, fueling concern in Seoul, and in Japan, that Washington is becoming a less dependable ally in the face of risingthreats from North KoreaandChina.

Iran's currency hit a record low against the dollar on Monday as the U.S. government prepared to unveil new sanctions in a bid to further squeeze the country's economy to force Iran's theocratic rulers to capitulate in the nearly six-month war.

The rial dropped to 2.02 million to the U.S. dollar as trading opened on informal currency markets. Iran's official Central Bank rate stood at around 1.5 million rials to the dollar, but the informal rate is what most Iranians pay.

The currency had already been under pressure before the U.S. and Israel attacked Iran on Feb. 28, with double-digit inflation and negative growth, but it has been hitting new lows as the war takes an even greater toll.

Greek military reportedly moves Patriot interceptor systems in response to Iranian threats

The Greek military on Monday moved one of its Patriot missile interceptor systems to the island of Crete in response to Iranian threats to hit U.S. military bases in Europe, local media reports said.

The battery was moved from the eastern island of Karpathos to Crete, where there is a major U.S. base at Souda, the reports said. Media images showed the Patriot system on a truck waiting to be loaded on a barge.

The Financial Times reportedlast week, quoting Iranian sources, that Iran would fire missiles at U.S. targets in Europe if President Donald Trump steps up military pressure over the Strait of Hormuz.

The Greek defense ministry did not comment on the reported transfer of the missile system.

Iran-Oman talks on Strait of Hormuz to continue Tuesday

Oman and Iran are set to continue their talks on the future management of the Strait of Hormuz on Tuesday, Tehran's foreign ministry spokesperson Esmail Baghaei said Monday.

The spokesperson said Oman's foreign minister will travel to Tehran to meet with Iranian officials.

The two countries, whose shorelines include the strategic waterway, have been engaged in talks for weeks to negotiate how to manage the chokepoint. Some details have emerged regarding the possible introduction of fees for ships transiting the strait, as well as specific routes for entering and exiting the area.

Treasury Secretary warns Iran, allies of "economic D-Day"

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has warned Iran and its allies the U.S. will unleash an "economic D-Day" on Iran as part of a shift in strategy to pile economic pressure in the hope of breaking the stalemate in the conflict – a term first used by President Trump last week.

Writing in the BritishFinancial Times newspaper, Bessent said the measures would be "the single greatest financial offensive ever marshaled against an adversary."

The secretary also urged Iran's allies to sever ties with Tehran, saying that doing so  would "deepen their access to global capital, reinforce confidence in their markets and attain the standing they seek in the world economy."

Bessent is set to hold a news conference on Monday at which he will unveil the new measures.

After months of military pressure and a failed agreement to negotiate an end to the war, Mr.  Trump signaled in recent weeks a shift in the strategy to exert economic pressure on Iran.

Iran's president defends memorandum of understanding as best path forward

Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian defended the memorandum of understanding signed with the U.S. in June as the best way for the country to move beyond what he described as a situation of "neither war nor peace."

In a speech published by state-run IRNA news agency Sunday, he said members of Iran's Supreme National Security Council believed the agreement was the best possible based on what he described as dignity, wisdom and the national interest.

"There is not a single provision in this agreement that amounts to capitulation," Pezeshkian said. "The supreme leader sets the policies, and we will follow that path."

The deal known as the Memorandum of Understanding was signed by President Trump in June. It set an ambitious 60-day deadline for ending the war with Iran and reaching an accord on its nuclear program. That period ended last week, after weeks of stalled progress and flare-ups, with both sides declaring they had abandoned it. There appear to be no signs of compromise on the crucial issue of reopening the Strait of Hormuz, vital to global shipping.

Iran says any country joining U.S. sanctions is considered an "enemy"

Iran on Saturday warned countries against participating in the United States' "economic war" against the Islamic Republic, saying they would be considered enemies.

"We declare to all countries ... do not join the economic war waged by the United States. Any country participating in the imposition of economic restrictions against us is considered an enemy," Mohsen Rezai, the head of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, said on state television.

If the countries concerned refuse to "distance themselves" from the United States, Iran would "harm" their interests, he added.

Rezai also insisted Iran had more cards to play in the war, claiming, "We have not yet attacked any American economic interests."

President Trump said  last week the U.S. will enforce "Economic Warfare and Isolation on an unprecedented scale." But he also called on the rest of the world to come to the aid of the U.S. in enforcing sanctions, the specifics of which have still not been announced.
