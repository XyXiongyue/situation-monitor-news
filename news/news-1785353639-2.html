Fed Holds Ground At 3.6% As Oil Spikes And Inflation Refuses To Budge

Wed, 29 Jul 2026 12:10

The Federal Reserve opted to keep its key interest rate unchanged Wednesday at around 3.6%, choosing to hold steady for a fifth consecutive meeting despite lingering inflation and energy market disruptions linked to the war in Iran.

The Fed’s rate-setting committee reached the decision in a 9-3 vote following two days of deliberations. Three policymakers—Beth Hammack of the Cleveland Fed, Neel Kashkari of the Minneapolis Fed, and Lorie Logan of the Dallas Fed—dissented, voting instead for a quarter-point rate hike to combat high prices.

The hold comes as inflation remains stuck above the central bank’s 2% target, where it has stayed for more than five years. Ongoing fighting in the Middle East has pushed energy costs higher, creating added uncertainty for the U.S. economic outlook.

Crude oil briefly passed $100 a barrel last week following the shutdown of the Strait of Hormuz after Feb. 28 military strikes. While prices have fluctuated since, average oil costs remain $10 to $15 higher per barrel than at this time last year.

Presiding over his second meeting, Fed Chair Kevin Warsh has stated that he has “no tolerance” for elevated inflation. Warsh was appointed by President Donald Trump, who has pressed the central bank to lower interest rates rather than raise them. Warsh is scheduled to hold a press conference at 2:30 p.m. ET.

Wall Street traders generally anticipated Wednesday’s hold, with only 33% predicting a rate increase. However, expectations for future action have shifted. Data from CME shows that 76% of traders now expect a rate hike in September, up from 59% a month ago.

Fed officials are waiting on key data releases before making further changes to monetary policy. On Thursday, the Commerce Department will release its first estimate of second-quarter economic growth, alongside June figures for the personal consumption expenditures (PCE) price index, the Fed’s preferred inflation metric.

U.S. inflation peaked above 9% in mid-2022 before dropping following 11 rate increases by the Fed in 2022 and 2023. Progress toward the 2% goal has since stalled. Alongside high energy costs, new tariffs on foreign goods and rising electricity demand from artificial intelligence data centers have maintained upward pressure on prices.

However, core inflation eased somewhat in June as apartment rent growth slowed.
