Will Lower Gas Prices Follow Trump's 'Biggest Oil Deal In World History'?

Sat, 29 Aug 2026 00:35

A sweeping new oil deal comes with a promise every driver wants to hear, but any payoff at the pump may be well down the road.

President Donald Trump announced the US-Venezuela oil agreement on Friday, Aug. 28, calling it the biggest oil deal in world history.

President Donald Trump announced the agreement between the US and Venezuela on Friday, Aug. 28, saying it would expand American access to oil and eventually cut gas prices.

“The United States of America has just entered into an Agreement with the Country of Venezuela on, THE BIGGEST OIL DEAL IN WORLD HISTORY!”Trump wrote in a Truth Social post.

The agreement gives US interests majority control over more than 65 billion barrels of proven Venezuelan oil reserves,according to ABC News.

Venezuela’s interim president, Delcy Rodriguez, said the agreement calls for more than $100 billion in investment and the development of 17 strategic oil fields.

“This Historic Transaction MORE THAN DOUBLES American Oil Reserves, greatly increases our Oil Supply, and will substantially lower Gas Prices for all Americans, long into the future,” Trump said.

The deal could place downward pressure on oil and gasoline prices if it leads to a large, sustained increase in production. However, neither government announced a timetable for when drivers could see savings.

Control of reserves does not mean that oil is immediately available to refiners or consumers.

Venezuela’s oil industry has struggled with aging equipment, underinvestment, sanctions, and deteriorating refineries. Much of the country’s crude is also extra-heavy and requires specialized processing, according to the US Energy Information Administration.

Restoring large-scale production will require extensive private investment and infrastructure work. That makes the deal more likely to influence prices over the long term than produce a sharp, immediate drop at neighborhood gas stations.

CBS News reportedthat a private joint venture received a 100-year concession covering fields with 65 billion barrels of oil. The US government will control 55 percent of the venture through equity and access to oil at cost, the outlet reported.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio said the agreement would benefit both countries.

“This deal is a huge win for both the American and Venezuelan people,”Rubio stated in a post on X. “It demonstrates how President Trump's bold foreign policy is driving America First wins: securing stable reserves and low-cost oil in our Hemisphere and lowering gas prices here at home.

“For the Venezuelan people, this deal will bring nearly $100 billion in private investment, support thousands of high-paying jobs, and drive the reconstruction of Venezuela's economy.”

The bottom line for drivers is that lower prices are possible, but not guaranteed.

Global demand, refinery capacity, infrastructure repairs, investment, and disruptions elsewhere in the oil market will help determine when, and whether, the deal produces noticeable savings.
