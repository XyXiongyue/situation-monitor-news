Geopolitical tensions, tight supply keep Asia petrochemical prices elevated

2026-08-28 18:28

inOil & Companies News28/08/2026

Most petrochemical markets in Asia have strengthened on constrained production amid rising feedstock costs, with supply expected to remain tight in September, while a cautious demand recovery is possible on China’s pre-holiday restocking and as monsoon season in India ends.

• Restocking likely in China ahead of week-long holiday in October• Ethylene derivative markets mixed; PE, MEG perform better• SM-led cost pressure lifts ABS prices despite weak consumption

From olefins to aromatics and other derivative markets, prices went on a general uptrend in the week ended 21 August, but there was no broad recovery in end-user consumption.

In the last week of August, markets continue to deal with elevated geopolitical tensions amid a deadlock in peace negotiations between the US and Iran nearly six months since their conflict started in the Middle East.

Petrochemical prices largely tracked crude oil prices in the current week, with Brent prices initially climbing above $90/barrel on 25 August before easing to around $87/barrel in early trade on Thursday.

Factors such as supply reductions, disrupted Middle East flows and higher replacement costs provide sellers greater leverage in the market, while buyers have stayed largely on the sidelines.

OLEFINS DERIVATIVE DEMAND LEND SUPPORTEthylene prices surged in the week ending 21 August, supported by demand from better-performing derivatives including ethylene oxide (EO), ethylene glycols, polyethylene (PE) and ethylene propylene diene monomer (EPDM).

However, the increase was not accompanied by widespread panic buying.

Demand from regularly importing derivatives such as polyvinyl chloride (PVC) and ethylene vinyl acetate (EVA) remained muted.

Ethylene producers were cautious about purchasing additional naphtha because of rising upstream costs and tight feedstock availability from some origins.

Propylene import prices in northeast Asia increased for two weeks running, supported by higher crude oil values, tight regional availability and gains in China’s domestic market.

Trading remained limited as elevated prices constrained affordability and kept many participants on the sidelines.

Some derivative producers reduced operating rates in response to deteriorating economics, with integrated producers considering lowering polypropylene (PP) production to release propylene for sale if propylene’s price uptrend persists.

In southeast Asia, propylene prices also firmed as regional spot cargoes remained scarce, although sluggish PP sales restricted buyers’ ability to accept higher offers.

Supply could improve in the second half of September if facilities in Malaysia and Thailand restart, but Indonesia’s Pertamina was not expected to restart its Balikpapan residual fluid catalytic cracking unit before October.

AROMATICS LIFTED BY HIGHER CRUDEHigher styrene monomer (SM) costs on crude gains pushed up prices of downstream acrylonitrile butadiene styrene (ABS) in Asia.

Regional ABS prices reached their highest levels in two to three months, with China’s domestic market recording the strongest gains.

Earlier production cuts in response to weaker prices and compressed margins also limited near-term ABS availability.

In the toluene market, prices were tracking fluctuations in upstream crude oil and feedstock naphtha markets this week while supply is crimped by curbs in refinery runs in China.

Meanwhile, China’s toluene exports in July more than doubled year on year, according to the ICIS Supply and Demand Database, amid strong overseas demand and favorable export arbitrage.

DERIVATIVESAsia’s monoethylene glycol (MEG) has closely tracked upstream crude prices. The market is facing acute supply tightness despite reduced demand following output cuts from downstream polyester and polyethylene terephthalate (PET) sectors.

Supply in August and September tightened because of reduced exports from the Middle East.

The price surge followed nearly an 8% rise in MEG prices in the week ending 21 August.

China’s domestic MEG supply declined as crude-based plants were unable to operate at optimal rates because of limited feedstock. Coal-based units, however, were running at full capacity on attractive production margins.

Downstream polyester and PET producers shut units or reduced output to manage the feedstock shortage.

For EVA, import prices in Asia were stable-to-firm as producers raised September discussions while attempting to transfer increased ethylene costs to buyers.

EVA availability was curtailed as several swing producers in northeast and southeast Asia reduced output or prioritized low-density polyethylene (LDPE) production.

For instance, Thailand’s TPI Polene was expected to operate its 93,000 tonne/year EVA/LDPE swing plant at Map Ta Phut at full LDPE production for about one month from late August or early September, according to market sources.

In the PVC market, restocking in India has improved recently as some pipe manufacturers have started restocking for an expected post-monsoon pickup, but higher container freight costs was the main driver for price increments.

METHANOL UP ON SUPPLY CONSTRAINTSMethanol prices also rose across Asia as Middle East supply remained constrained by unresolved US-Iran conflict.

Southeast Asian prices increased amid tight availability – a Malaysian plant is shut for a turnaround, while another plant recently restarted following a brief outage.

Prices in Taiwan and South Korea followed the wider regional increase, although spot demand remained limited because buyers were largely covered by term volumes.

In China, methanol values rose alongside spiking domestic futures prices.

Demand may receive seasonal support in September as Chinese buyers typically procure material ahead of the country’s 1-7 October National Day holiday.
