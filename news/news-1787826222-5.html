SK Gas Pays Record Panama Canal Transit Fee

2026-08-27 18:23

inInternational Shipping News27/08/2026

The “express transit fee” paid by a single vessel to pass through the Panama Canal has surpassed 7 billion won.

SK Gas has agreed to pay $5.30 million (about 7.3 billion won) for passage through the Panama Canal on September 1, marking the highest amount ever, Bloomberg reported on August 26 local time. The figure breaks the previous record of $4.60 million (about 6.4 billion won) set by SK Shipping earlier this month, only a few weeks later. Through the successful auction bid, SK Gas’s liquefied petroleum gas, or LPG, carrier G. Spirit secured the right to transit northbound through the canal.

The sharp rise in transit fees reflects a combination of geopolitical risk and climate change. In the aftermath of the Iran war, major trade routes from the West to Asia have contracted, driving more vessels toward the Panama Canal. According to data analytics firm Argus Media, the waiting time for ships arriving at the canal without a reservation has now stretched to as long as 11 days.

Natural disasters are also hindering operations. The El Niño phenomenon in the Pacific has caused severe drought in the Panama region, lowering the freshwater levels needed to operate the canal. As a result, the Panama Canal Authority plans to further tighten limits from September on the number of transit slots issued and on vessel load capacity. With supply shrinking and demand rising, the resulting supply-demand imbalance has led auction prices to surge.

The amount SK Gas is paying this time is effectively a separate kind of “express fee” added on top of the regular toll. It is determined through an auction system joined by vessels that failed to secure reservations in time or require urgent passage. Commenting on recent auction results, the Panama Canal Authority said they “show that demand has become considerably stronger.” Considering that the average auction price before February had been around $55,000, the winning bid has jumped nearly 100-fold in just half a year.

G. Spirit is currently waiting on the Pacific side of the Panama Canal for passage. SK Gas did not respond to Bloomberg’s request for comment regarding the auction result. As logistics uncertainty grows, companies’ efforts to shorten canal transit times even at enormous cost are likely to continue for the time being.Source: Business Korea
