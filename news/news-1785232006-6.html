Oil price outlook: OPEC+ supply hike and weak demand signal further downside

2026-07-28 17:46

inOil & Companies News28/07/2026

Oil just suffered a notable plunge—Brent dropped 6.8% and WTI fell 8.2% in a single day, wiping out weeks of bullish momentum. The big question: is this the start of a deeper slide, or a short-term shakeout?

Geopolitics vs. Supply: Oil’s Tug of WarThe latest tumble in Brent Oil (LCO) and Crude Oil (CL) prices was triggered by a pause in U.S.-Iran hostilities, which cooled fears of supply disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz. That relief sent oil back toward one-week lows, erasing a surge fueled by Middle East tensions and Red Sea shipping risks. Read more

OPEC+ is now expected to raise output quotas in September by 188,000 barrels per day, potentially adding more supply to a market already wrestling with demand doubts. Read more

Demand Warning SignsThe U.S. Energy Information Administration reported a surprise 2.01M barrel build in crude inventories—when the market expected a draw. This signals weaker demand or oversupply, both factors that could weigh on prices further. Read more

Meanwhile, BofA warns that oil’s volatility—not just its price—could stoke inflation, especially if prices bounce but don’t fall as quickly. That makes the macro backdrop more unpredictable. Read more

Price Action: A Technical BreakdownBrent: Last at $85.42, down from a 52-week high of $126.41WTI: Last at $82.01, well off its $117.63 highBoth have erased much of their year-to-date gains in a matter of days

Positioning and What’s NextSpeculators are still net long U.S. crude (81.7K contracts), but that’s down from 110K+ just weeks ago, signaling fading conviction as volatility rises. If OPEC+ follows through on output hikes, and demand stays soft, the path of least resistance could be lower—unless a new geopolitical shock reignites supply fears.
