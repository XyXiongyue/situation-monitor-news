Stark data shows Red Sea shipping plummeting as Houthi strikes rise

Mon, 27 Jul 2026 11:17

Houthi attacks on Saudi oil depots along the Red Sea on Sunday resulted in a drop in ship traffic through the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, data from analytics platform Kpler revealed on Monday, according to reporting from The Hill.

Only 11 cargo vessels moved through thestrait, the lowest number in months, according to Kpler's data.

According to the data, three of the seven oil vessels entered theRed Sea, while two were headed to the Saudi port of Yanbu to load Saudi crude.

A Russian-linked ship, while four others carrying 2 million barrels of Saudi and Emirati crude, including a Hong Kong-flagged crude carrier, passed through the Strait. A tanker carrying around 750,000 barrels of Saudi crude was headed for Pakistan.

The Houthi militant group in Yemen claimed on Wednesday that it targeted two Saudi oil tankers with missiles and drones in the Red Sea.

TheHouthisare armed and significantly influenced by Iran but are independent of Iran's supreme leader, unlike the Lebanese militantHezbollah groupand several Iran-backed Iraqi militias.

In a statement, the group said the Encelia and the Layla were violating the blockade and claimed that the strikes had caused large fires on both vessels.

Saudi Arabia said it had shot down drones fired by Iran-backed militias in Iraq that had targeted its petroleum facilities. Iran-backed Houthi rebels in Yemen, meanwhile, claimed to have attacked Saudi oil facilities as part ofa separate but related conflict. It was unclear if they referred to the same attack.

On Monday, international benchmark Brent crude oil dipped to $87.59 a barrel at one point, having surged to around $101 a barrel on Thursday. West Texas Intermediate, the North American benchmark, dropped to more than $83 a barrel.

According to the U.S. Energy Information Administration, around 8 percent of the world's oil passes through the Bab el-Mandeb Strait at the Red Sea's southern end.
