From Awareness to Action: Where Maritime Domain Awareness Is Heading

2026-07-31 18:10

inInternational Shipping News31/07/2026

Speed-to-Understanding Is the Dominant DemandFor much of the past decade, the AI conversation in maritime intelligence focused on accuracy and coverage. In 2026, the conversation has shifted to latency and comprehension. A capability that produces the right answer 72 hours after the operational window has closed is no longer valuable in the way it once was. A capability that produces a usable answer in the window the commander is actually working in is what the community is now asking for.

Data must be processed fast and understood by people. Intelligence must arrive at the speed the commander wants. AI is the way to keep up with growing data volume, but the operational value is measured in how quickly understanding follows.

The operational reality behind the demand is visible in the data. Windward tracked 2,157 unique cargo ships and tankers over 10,000 DWT conducting at least one prolonged dark activity event in Q2 2026, nearly five times the 449 recorded in Q1. The number of prolonged dark activity events rose from 451 to 3,137, a sevenfold increase quarter over quarter. Each of those events represents a moment where AIS-based situational awareness gave way to something less certain, and where the community’s ability to understand what was happening had to come from somewhere other than the primary broadcast signal.

Maritime operations in 2026 increasingly happen in environments where the connectivity assumptions of shore-based intelligence do not hold. Contested electromagnetic environments, GPS jamming, degraded satellite communications, and the geographic reach of forward-deployed units all mean that intelligence capabilities need to work with limited or intermittent bandwidth to the enterprise.

This changes what “deploying AI” actually means. It is not about pushing more processing to the cloud. It is about pushing enough processing to the edge that operators can act on what they see without waiting for the connectivity that may not arrive.

For the vendor community, the implication is direct. Solutions that work only when full-bandwidth connectivity to enterprise systems is available are increasingly a partial answer. Solutions that continue to work when connectivity is degraded are the operational answer the community is asking for.

From Awareness to Dominance and ActionOne of the more striking pattern shifts in the community’s vocabulary is the language itself. Maritime Domain Awareness, the term the community has used for decades, is being joined by two others.

Maritime Domain Dominance frames MDA as necessary but insufficient.

Maritime Domain Action captures the same analytical pivot from a slightly different operational angle. The shared observation across both framings is that the community is no longer treating awareness as the destination. Awareness is the prerequisite. What operators, commanders, and agencies are being asked to do is act on the awareness, deny adversary freedom of operation, and reshape the operating environment through the intelligence advantage the community has been building.

This is a language shift that reflects an operational one. Building the picture is not the same as using it. The demand signals across the community are consistently about using the picture, not about seeing it more clearly.

Trust and Adoption Are the Friction PointsThe most honest conversations in maritime intelligence in 2026 are about the human side of the AI adoption question. Commanders and operators raise the same set of concerns repeatedly: how operators build trust in AI models, whether that trust exists before capabilities are rolled out, why operators often do not use the AI tools already available to them, and the training gaps that keep systems from being fully adopted even after deployment.

Capability alone is not the barrier. In many cases, the capability exists, has been procured, and is available in the operational environment. The bottleneck is confidence in what the system is doing, transparency about its reasoning, and the training that would allow operators to interrogate its outputs the way they interrogate a human analyst’s.

Bob Sharp, Windward’s Chief Maritime Intelligence Officer, has put the urgency in strategic context. Build AI fast, adopt fast, use fast, and the operational advantage compounds. Fall behind on any of those three, and adversaries investing heavily in the same capabilities will close the gap. The strategic stake, in his framing, is not whether the community builds AI. It is whether the community can build, adopt, and use AI at a pace that stays ahead of the adversaries doing the same.

For the community serving maritime security, this places training, explainability, and operator interface design at the same level of strategic importance as the underlying models themselves. A model that operators do not trust is a capability that goes unused, regardless of what it can technically do.

The Concrete Demand SignalsAcross the maritime security community, a set of specific operational asks has come into focus. Each maps to a distinct piece of the operational reality the community is trying to address.

Vessel Identity Verification Against Old Ships and Reused NumbersThe persistence of vessels operating under fraudulent identities remains a live problem. In Q2 2026, 275 internationally trading tankers were broadcasting the flag of a fraudulent registry. Windward has now identified 22 fraudulent ship registries, with Russia-linked tankers falsely claiming Syria and Myanmar flags for the first time during the quarter. Around 90% of tankers using fraudulent registries are Western-sanctioned. The IMO database now lists 580 falsely flagged vessels, up from 470 at the end of 2025.

Small-Boat TrackingThe operational surface for small-boat activity is not covered by AIS in the same way commercial shipping is. Multi-sensor intelligence combining SAR, EO, and RF is where the community sees the operational answer.

GPS Jamming ResponseThe persistence of GPS jamming across multiple maritime regions is now a structural operational condition. Windward tracked 171,286 distinct vessels affected by jamming at least once globally in Q2 2026 and detected 3.35 million false ship-to-ship meetings since Operation Epic Fury began on February 28, 2026, in which injected positioning coordinates made vessels appear to rendezvous when no meeting took place.

Target-Quality Common Operational PictureData fusion across AIS, satellite imagery, RF, and behavioral context into a single fused picture is now framed as the operational requirement, not the aspiration.

Trade DataUnderstanding vessel activity in the context of the trade it is enabling is where the intelligence picture gets sharpest. The linkage between vessel behavior and the commercial and geopolitical activity behind it is what turns tracking into intelligence.Subsea Cable Protection

In Q2 2026, Windward recorded 3,667 loitering events exceeding 12 hours above subsea cables, involving 2,206 cargo and tanker vessels. The Red Sea and Gulf of Aden concentrated 21% of all recorded events. The threat picture is not episodic. It is a sustained pattern that demands sustained monitoring.

Narcotics Interdiction and Container-Level IntelligenceMultiple agencies across the interdiction community need behavioral and identity intelligence at the container and vessel level, particularly in the operational environments where the surface is dense with both legitimate and illegitimate traffic.

The demand signals share a common thread. Each represents a use case where the community already understands the operational problem and is asking for the analytical infrastructure to solve it at the pace the operational environment demands.

Where Windward Comes InFor Windward, these demand signals map directly to the capabilities we have been building. Multi-Sensor Intelligence delivers the fused operational picture across AIS, SAR, EO, RF, and behavioral context that the target-quality COP conversation is asking for. Know Your Vessel (KYV™) supports the vessel identity verification that the fraudulent-flag environment requires. Critical Maritime Infrastructure Protection supports the cable-proximate monitoring the community is asking for. And GPS Jamming Resilience provides the operational answer to the environmental compromise of AIS-based situational awareness.

The presence of a vessel in a GPS jamming-affected area is not, by itself, a behavioral risk indicator. GPS jamming is something happening to a geographic area, not something a vessel is doing. Vessel risk is assessed independently based on behavioral patterns and operating history.

Where This Is HeadingThe maritime security community has clearer language for what it is trying to do and sharper specificity about what it needs from the intelligence community, the vendor community, and the training and adoption architecture that connects both.Speed matters more than ever. The edge is where the operational value increasingly sits. Awareness is no longer the destination. Trust is the human friction point that determines whether capability translates into operational advantage. And the demand signals are specific enough that the community can now measure vendor and capability alignment against them directly.Source: Windward
