India calls for immediate de-escalation in West Asia, condemns Hormuz shipping attacks as ‘deeply concerning’

2026-07-30 18:18

inInternational Shipping News30/07/2026

India has called for an immediate de-escalation of tensions in West Asia following a renewed escalation of hostilities in the region, while strongly condemning attacks on commercial vessels passing through the Strait of Hormuz.

Addressing a United Nations Security Council open debate on the situation in the Middle East on Tuesday, India’s Permanent Representative to the UN, Ambassador Parvathaneni Harish, said the resurgence of violence after a brief pause in the conflict was “deeply concerning” and urged all sides to exercise restraint.

“After a brief pause in the ongoing conflict in the region, there has been a worrying resumption of attacks and escalation of hostilities. This is deeply concerning and India calls for an immediate de-escalation of tensions,” Harish said.

India specifically condemned attacks on vessels including GFS Galaxy, MT Al Bahiyah and MT Mombasa while they were transiting through the Strait of Hormuz earlier this month. The incidents affected Indian nationals, with one Indian killed, another reported missing and several others injured.

“India has consistently condemned acts of violence targeting seafarers and disrupting free and safe navigation through international waterways like the Strait of Hormuz,” Harish said.

Calling for the restoration of maritime security, he added, “The targeting of commercial shipping and civilian infrastructure in the region must end, and free and unimpeded navigation and commerce through the international waterways in the region, in keeping with international law, must be restored at the earliest.”

The remarks came amid continuing tensions involving the US, Israel and Iran. According to the United Nations Economic and Social Commission for Asia and the Pacific, Iran declared the Strait of Hormuz closed again on July 11 after attacks on commercial vessels began earlier in the month. Vessel movements through the strategic waterway dropped sharply from pre-conflict levels.

Highlighting India’s economic and strategic interests in West Asia, Harish said the region remains crucial for India’s trade, energy security and diaspora. He noted that India has annual bilateral trade of around $180 billion with the region, cumulative foreign direct investment exceeding $31 billion, and receives more than $52 billion in remittances from Gulf Cooperation Council countries.

Nearly 10 million Indians live and work in the Gulf region, Harish said, adding, “Their safety and well-being are a key priority for us.”

India also urged the international community not to overlook the humanitarian situation in Gaza amid the focus on developments in the Strait of Hormuz.

“The loss of civilian lives and destruction of civilian infrastructure are pressing concerns that the international community must act on immediately, with a deep sense of urgency,” Harish said.

Reaffirming India’s long-standing position on the Israel-Palestine issue, he said New Delhi supports a two-state solution, with “a sovereign, independent and viable State of Palestine living side by side in peace with Israel, within secure and recognised borders.”

Harish also said India remains committed to supporting Lebanon’s sovereignty and condemned attacks on United Nations Interim Force in Lebanon (UNIFIL) peacekeepers. He added that protecting maritime routes, including the Bab al-Mandab Strait and the southern Red Sea, remains a shared international responsibility.Source: Moneycontrol
