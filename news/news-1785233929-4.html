Houthis Reignite Tensions With Red Sea Shipping Attack

2026-07-28 18:18

inInternational Shipping News28/07/2026

The Iran-backed Houthi movement has sharply escalated tensions in the Middle East by targeting Saudi-linked shipping in the Red Sea and declaring a naval blockade, bringing renewed attention to a conflict that had remained relatively contained in recent months.

The latest attacks have raised fears over global energy supplies and maritime trade, according to The New York Times. The group says it has launched missile and drone strikes against oil tankers, while a Saudi-led military coalition has responded with airstrikes inside Yemen.

The developments signal a significant shift in the regional conflict. Until now, the Houthis had largely stayed on the sidelines of the broader confrontation involving Iran and the United States.

Instead, the group has now chosen to directly challenge Saudi Arabia, a key American ally, reopening a rivalry that had eased following years of conflict.

The Houthis are a Shiite militant movement that has fought Yemen’s internationally recognized government for nearly two decades.

They seized the capital, Sanaa, in 2014, forcing the government to relocate south.

A year later, Saudi Arabia led a military coalition aimed at removing the group from power. Years of Saudi-led airstrikes failed to drive the Houthis out of northern Yemen.

The war has devastated Yemen, leaving millions short of food and basic healthcare, while years of fighting have deepened one of the world’s worst humanitarian crises.

A UN-brokered ceasefire in 2022 largely halted the fighting between the Houthis and Saudi Arabia but they have not reached a permanent peace agreement, and Yemen remains divided.

The Bab al-Mandab strait links the Red Sea to the Gulf of Aden, making it a key passage for global trade and for oil and gas shipments moving between Europe, Asia and the Middle East.

During the Gaza war, the Houthis repeatedly targeted commercial shipping in the Red Sea, saying they were acting in support of Palestinians.

Now, with the Strait of Hormuz facing prolonged disruption, the Red Sea has become even more important for Saudi energy exports.

Any sustained attacks on vessels using this route could increase pressure on international shipping, insurance costs and energy markets.

Iran connection with its own limits

The Houthis are widely viewed as one of Iran’s closest regional partners, but analysts say the relationship is more complex than simple control.

Iran has helped transform the movement from a local insurgency into a heavily armed force equipped with ballistic missiles, cruise missiles and long-range drones.

Even so, the Houthis continue to make decisions based on their own political and military interests inside Yemen.

Recent statements by Iranian officials have also suggested growing acceptance of the Houthis’ ambitions to become a lasting regional power.

The latest escalation has added fresh uncertainty across the Middle East, where several regional conflicts are still unresolved.Saudi Arabia is now under pressure on both its security and its shipping routes, while the Houthis seem intent on using force to improve their bargaining position.

What happens next will depend on how both sides react in the days and weeks ahead.

For now, the Red Sea is back in the spotlight as one of the region’s most sensitive flashpoints, with risks that reach far beyond Yemen.Source: MoneyControl
