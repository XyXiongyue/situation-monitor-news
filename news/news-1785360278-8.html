Oil rises 5% as U.S.-Saudi strikes in Iraq revive Middle East supply fears

2026-07-30 05:24

inOil & Companies News30/07/2026

Investing.com — Oil prices extended their gains on Wednesday after the United States and Saudi Arabia launched joint strikes against Iran-backed groups in Iraq, while fresh tensions in the Middle East and a sharp draw in U.S. crude inventories supported sentiment.

The interception of a fresh barrage of Iranian ballistic missiles targeting U.S. forces also reinforced concerns that the recent pause in hostilities could prove short-lived.

As of 07:25 ET (11:25 GMT), Brent Oil Futures rose 5.1% to $88.40 a barrel, while Crude Oil WTI Futures gained 4.8% to $83.10 a barrel, recovering after both benchmarks had retreated roughly 15% over the previous three sessions.

Middle East risks remain firmly in focus

The latest gains came after the United States and Saudi Arabia carried out strikes against Iran-backed groups in Iraq, blaming them for recent drone attacks on Saudi oil facilities. Tehran rejected the accusations, warning that linking Iran to the attacks represented a “major miscalculation.”

The strikes followed the U.S. military’s interception of multiple Iranian ballistic missiles targeting American forces in the region, reinforcing concerns that the recent pause in hostilities could quickly unravel.

Markets had earlier grown optimistic that diplomacy could gain traction following Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu’s meeting with U.S. President Donald Trump in Washington.

Trump later said there was a good chance talks with Iran would make progress, although Tehran has denied seeking negotiations or a ceasefire.

ANZ analysts said efforts to reopen the Strait of Hormuz have made little progress after Iran rejected an Omani proposal, backed by Gulf states, that would have created a joint framework for managing traffic through the strategic waterway.

The bank added that tanker traffic through Hormuz remained subdued, with just five commodity vessels transiting the strait on Tuesday, while threats to regional energy infrastructure remained elevated after recent attacks on Saudi oil facilities.

Inventories, OPEC+ help crude recover from three-day slide

Oil found support after the American Petroleum Institute reportedly estimated that U.S. crude inventories fell by about 3.3 million barrels last week, signalling resilient demand ahead of official government data due later on Wednesday.

Prices were also underpinned by reports that OPEC+ is considering pausing further production increases for three months from October, after completing the planned return of voluntary output cuts.

The recovery follows a sharp three-day selloff that saw WTI retreat roughly 15% from Friday’s intraday peak near $93.50, as traders unwound bullish bets amid hopes diplomatic efforts could ease tensions between Washington and Tehran before renewed military developments revived supply concerns.Source: Investing.com
